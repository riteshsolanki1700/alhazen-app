Formulas (Cartesian sign convention)

Lens: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f, m = v/u
Mirror: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f, m = −v/u

Key rules:
• Real images: formed by actual ray intersection.
• Virtual images: rays appear to diverge from behind/within.
• Try: u > 2f, u = 2f, u = f, and u < f to observe real vs virtual transitions.

Concepts

Snell's law: n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r.
Total Internal Reflection (TIR) occurs only when light goes from denser to rarer medium with i > i꜀, where sin i꜀ = n₂/n₁.

Optical Fibres: Rely completely on successive TIR along a glass core.

Mirror formula (derivation)

For a small aperture mirror, the object AB and its image A′B′ lie on the axis. Ray AM travels parallel to the axis and reflects through F. Ray AP hits the pole and reflects with equal angles. P′ is the foot of the perpendicular from M, and because the aperture is small P′F ≈ PF.

1. ΔA′B′F ~ ΔMP′F, so MP′/A′B′ = P′F/B′F ≈ PF/B′F.

2. MP′ = AB, therefore AB/A′B′ = PF/B′F.

3. ΔABP ~ ΔA′B′P, therefore AB/A′B′ = PB/PB′.

4. Equate: PB/PB′ = PF/B′F = PF/(PB′ − PF).

5. Apply signs: PB = −u, PB′ = −v, PF = −f:

u/v = f/(v − f)

6. Cross-multiply: uv − uf = fv, so uv = f(u + v). Divide by uvf:

1/f = 1/v + 1/u

Equivalent form: 1/u = 1/f − 1/v. Valid for small apertures, with the Cartesian sign convention.

Focal length and radius of curvature (f = R/2)

A ray parallel to the axis strikes the mirror at A. The line CA is the normal, so the angle of incidence is θ = ∠ACP. By the reflection law the reflected ray makes the same angle θ with the normal and passes through F.

The angle at C: θ = arc(AP)/R. The angle at F is the exterior angle of the triangle CAF, so φ = θ + θ = 2θ. For a small aperture φ = arc(AP)/PF.

So arc(AP)/PF = 2·arc(AP)/R, which gives 1/PF = 2/R.

f = R/2    R = 2f

The result holds only for paraxial rays (small aperture).

Linear magnification of a mirror

Linear (transverse) magnification is the ratio of image size to object size, with heights measured upward from the axis. A real, inverted image has a negative height, so m is negative.

m = hI/hO = −v/u

In terms of f:

m = −v/u = (f − v)/f = f/(f − u)

m has no unit. |m| > 1 means magnified, |m| < 1 diminished, m > 0 erect (virtual), m < 0 inverted (real).

Areal (superficial) magnification

For a small flat object lying perpendicular to the axis, both its length and breadth scale by m, so its area scales by m².

mA = image area / object area = (hI/hO)² = (v/u)² = m²
Image velocity along the axis

Start from the mirror formula in the form vu = f(u + v) and differentiate with respect to time (f is constant):

dv/dt = −m² · du/dt    |dv/dt| = m²|du/dt|

The image velocity (along the axis) is m² times the object velocity and has the opposite sign in Cartesian coordinates.

Refraction: terms and rays

Refraction is the change of direction of a ray when it passes from one medium into another.

Optically denser means higher refractive index (light is slower); optically rarer means lower. A ray bends towards the normal when entering a denser medium and away from it when entering a rarer one.

Snell's law and refractive index

Light of frequency ν has speed v = νλ in a medium. Frequency does not change at boundaries, only v and λ do: v₁ = νλ₁ and v₂ = νλ₂.

sin i / sin r = 1μ2 = v₁/v₂ = λ₁/λ₂

Absolute refractive index compares with vacuum: μ = c/v, so μ₁ = c/v₁ and μ₂ = c/v₂:

μ₁ sin i = μ₂ sin r
Total internal reflection and Snell's window

Going from denser to rarer, the refracted ray bends away from the normal. At critical angle C, r = 90°. For any i > C, all light reflects internally.

sin C = 1/μ

Snell's window: A source at depth d sends rays emerging through a circular patch of radius r:

r = d / √(μ² − 1)

For water (μ = 4/3), r ≈ 1.13 d.

Lens equation and lens maker's formula

A thin lens has two surfaces with radii R₁ and R₂. It sits in medium μ₁ and is made of material μ₂.

1/f = (μ₂/μ₁ − 1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂)
1/v − 1/u = 1/f
Prism formula (derivation)

A ray enters at Q at angle i and leaves at S at angle e.

A = r₁ + r₂
δ = i + e − A

At minimum deviation δₘ, i = e and r₁ = r₂ = A/2:

μ = sin[(A + δm)/2] / sin(A/2)
Fermat's principle

Light takes the route that makes travel time stationary: stationary optical path length ∫μ ds. From this single principle, both the law of reflection (i = r) and Snell's law (μ₁ sin i = μ₂ sin r) are derived.

Rayleigh scattering

Particles smaller than λ scatter light inversely as the fourth power of wavelength:

I ∝ 1/λ⁴

Blue light (~450 nm) scatters nearly 6 times more than red (~700 nm), causing blue skies and red sunsets.
